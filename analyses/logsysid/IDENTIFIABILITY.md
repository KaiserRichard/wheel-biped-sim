# What P3 can identify

This report separates structural ambiguity from poor recovery in a particular experiment. `REHEARSAL.md` and `rehearsal_results.json` contain seeded synthetic recovery errors and tested durations. These are software checks, not hardware validation.

| Quantity | Observable experiment / model | What remains ambiguous |
|---|---|---|
| Torque scale `k` | Lever: measured `F*r = k*tau_fb + offset + hysteresis`. Multiple levels and up/down sweeps separate slope and additive terms. Rehearsal assumes settled feedback equals command. | Lever-length/force calibration errors are systematic. `Kt = k*Kt_fw` only if `Kt_fw` and its torque-producing current convention are known. With no calibrated current, call the result torque scale, not measured N*m/A. |
| Wheel friction `Tc,b` | Steady plateaus: `k*tau_fb = Tc*sign(w) + b*w`. Multiple speed magnitudes give independent columns; both signs expose offsets. | One speed magnitude makes columns dependent. Cogging, Stribeck behaviour and temperature are omitted from the fitted form. This is free-drive friction, not contact friction. |
| Inertia and gain | Free spin: `dw/dt = (k/J)*tau_fb - (Tc/J)*sign(w) - (b/J)*w`. | Scaling `(k,J,Tc,b)` together leaves the speed response unchanged. Spin alone identifies ratios; independently measured `k` and friction provide scale. The slow-spin joint fit has correlated friction estimates; this is practical poor conditioning, not proof of structural non-identifiability with every possible excitation. |
| Motor lag and delay | Output-error fit of delayed first-order command response to torque feedback, using many transitions. | RX timestamp is reception, not necessarily internal measurement time. The fitted delay includes unknown feedback transport/filter effects. Delay and lag trade off; correlation in the report is local and model-dependent. Speed integration suppresses fast details. |
| Gyro turn-on bias | Mean during verified rest, with known zero angular rate. | Constant true rotation is indistinguishable from bias. Allan variance removes a constant mean and cannot estimate turn-on bias itself. Multiple power cycles are needed for the bias distribution. |
| Acceleration bias | Stationary mean minus known projected gravity; multiple surveyed orientations. | One unknown tilt confounds transverse bias and gravity projection; scale and misalignment need multiple orientations. The synthetic scalar acceleration noise has known mean removed. |
| White noise `N`, rate random walk `K` | Overlapping Allan variance: `sigma_A²=N²/tau + K²*tau/3`. White noise gives deviation slope -1/2, RRW +1/2. | Short records may not show an RRW region; a zero fitted K is not evidence of zero drift. Flat bias instability, thermal drift, quantisation, vibration and filtering need their own evidence. N/K-only fitting does not validate those omitted terms. |
| Sample-to-command delay | Modular timestamp subtraction plus corresponding TX time inside the same control cycle. | Clock stamps must share one timer, and CTRL must identify the exact raw sample used. Method A excludes attitude-filter phase, CAN arbitration after FIFO enqueue, motor internal delay and torque rise time. Add measured contributions to construct a full loop budget. |
| Regression delay | Rich gyro/angle motion and torque history; nuisance constant/trend fitted. | Other correlated controller states, clipping, closed-loop feedback and unknown filters can bias the estimate. A single sinusoid or near-static trace may not identify a unique lag. |

The theoretical bias averaging window `sqrt(3)*N/K` minimizes error relative to the initial bias in the particular white-noise + random-walk model. It is not a universal warm-up period. At board-like white noise the rehearsal predicts about 12 s, compared with roughly 0.55 s at the Studio gyro noise. Temperature stabilization is a separate issue.

Allan data is simulated at 100 Hz to bound runtime, preserving `N = sigma_1kHz*sqrt(0.001)` and the random-walk increment scale. The continuum RRW formula is an approximation at the shortest averaging times; neither the true hardware bandwidth nor decimation aliasing is simulated. Analyse actual data at its recorded rate or use documented anti-alias filtering before decimation. Overlapping Allan points are correlated; weighted least-squares standard errors are approximate, not independent-point confidence intervals. Empirical p90 from 5–12 seeds is a planning result, not a population guarantee.

The section 4 step duration sweep uses true friction as fixed input to isolate step-fit recovery. Section 8 adds the more practical chain: fit lever, fit independent friction plateaus using that scale, then fit a separate 5 s spin run. It propagates force/length noise and friction fitting error through to `J = k/(k/J)`; it still assumes the correct model family. Calibration and held-out data use distinct draws from documented seeds.

Synchronization was corrected during completion: the original smooth-trace alignment searched only ±0.1 s although the true offset was 0.23711 s. Its old -136,610 us error therefore did not demonstrate a lack of excitation. The regenerated report searches ±0.35 s and reports the actual error. Use EVENT as the primary anchor; weak-motion alignment still needs an independent check. Sub-microsecond EVENT agreement in the synthetic round trip checks bookkeeping, not physical synchronization precision.

## Completion boundary

The current request's Python importer, tests, synthetic rehearsal, duration estimates and bench procedure are complete when `verification.json` says PASS. The broader measured-data acceptance criterion also requires importing and fitting one real stationary IMU log. No such raw log is supplied in this package. `gyro_board` uses a published-in-the-local-reference standard-deviation number with ASSUMED drift; it is synthetic, not a reanalysis of a measured log. That hardware gate remains OPEN. No robot gains or Studio nominal parameter files were changed.
